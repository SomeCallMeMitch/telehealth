# Simple Freakin' Care — Reseller Profile

Last checked: September 15, 2026

## Bottom line

Simple Freakin' Care is useful as a live example of LegUp/National Telehealth Providers positioning and pricing, but it is **not evidence of proven scale**. Public launch posts say the brand officially launched on **September 14, 2026**, one day before this review.

The correct interpretation is therefore:

- good source for current messaging, funnel structure, and retail-price testing;
- too new for defensible conclusions about CAC, churn, subscriber volume, revenue, or product-market fit;
- not a valid benchmark for an established consumer telehealth operator.

## Launch and operating entity

Public launch posts state:

- official launch date: **September 14, 2026**;
- the brand is operated by **Swaim Holding Group LLC**, described publicly as a North Carolina LLC;
- the launch was promoted locally in Yadkin County, North Carolina;
- public social posts associate **Jaimi Kayla Swaim** and **Coy Swaim** with the brand.

The exact LLC ownership and management roles have not yet been independently confirmed from an authoritative North Carolina filing. The public evidence supports describing Jaimi and Coy Swaim as associated promoters/operators, not yet as legally verified owners.

The site's Terms page shows a December 29, 2025 revision date, but those terms are LegUp/NTP template language and should not be treated as the brand's commercial launch date.

## Offer and funnel

Current website positioning:

- consumer-facing virtual healthcare membership;
- nationwide availability;
- Primary Care + Prescription Discount Program at **$59/month**;
- 1,000+ medications promoted as free at participating local pharmacies when prescribed;
- no-insurance-needed positioning;
- care fulfillment disclosed as Leg Up Recovery / National Telehealth Providers;
- terms describe Simple Freakin' Care as a referral partner rather than the clinical provider.

Earlier observation in this project found a family offer around **$159/month**, covering up to eight people. That price should be rechecked periodically because the site and managed catalog may change.

## Public distribution signals

### Social

Publicly discoverable accounts include:

- Facebook: Simple Freakin' Care / @SimpleFreakinCare
- Instagram: @simplefreakincare

Launch promotion appears founder-led and locally oriented. Search results show repeated launch messaging, local community posting, and personal-account amplification.

### Paid advertising

No reliable public evidence of meaningful Meta, Google, or TikTok ad spend was established during this pass. Search-engine discovery of a social page or post is not the same as proof of an active paid campaign. Meta Ad Library status should be checked directly again after the brand has been live long enough to run campaigns.

### Organic search, traffic, backlinks, and reviews

Because the brand launched September 14, 2026:

- third-party traffic estimates would be unreliable or below reporting thresholds;
- search visibility and backlink counts have not had time to mature;
- review volume cannot yet establish customer experience at scale;
- no defensible subscriber or revenue estimate can be made.

Do not infer zero customers from the absence of third-party estimates, but do not use the website as evidence of traction.

### Employer, group, or affiliate sales

No clear employer/group program or public affiliate program was found on the current Simple Freakin' Care site during this pass. Its visible funnel is primarily direct-to-consumer.

## Comparison with other LegUp/NTP resellers

### Fit For Duty Rx

Fit For Duty Rx is a stronger example of a deliberate B2B/employer strategy, but it also appears small:

- LinkedIn lists it as founded in 2025;
- LinkedIn showed 1 visible employee and 14 followers when checked;
- founder/CEO identified publicly as Lou Tornabeni;
- messaging explicitly targets employers, first responders, EMS/public-safety organizations, independent living communities, and co-branded workforce pages;
- public posts pitch employer access with no employer setup cost, while employees enroll directly.

This is useful channel-design evidence, not proof of large member volume.

### Drip Hydration & Wellness Bar

Drip illustrates the existing-audience cross-sell model. The current managed site advertises Primary Care at **$45/month**, Urgent Care at **$28/month**, Behavioral Health + Rx at **$21/month**, Dermatology at **$35/month**, and Vet Care at **$21/month**. Public evidence associates Jennifer Clifton with the business. No credible telehealth subscriber count was found.

### EZE Optimal Health / EZE Élitaire

EZE is a broader wellness and diagnostics ecosystem that added telehealth through National Telehealth Providers / LegUp. Its public virtual-care page was indexed in July 2026, and social search results show giveaway-driven promotion. This is another example of attaching telehealth to an existing brand, but no meaningful telehealth membership count was found.

## Commercial implications

1. **Simple Freakin' Care should be removed from the “market validation” column.** It validates that a partner can launch the offer and price it at $59, not that consumers buy or retain it.
2. **The existing-audience hypothesis gets stronger.** Fit For Duty, Drip, and EZE all lean on an existing founder story, audience, employer niche, or wellness operation.
3. **The market still lacks a verified breakout reseller.** That could mean opportunity, but it could also mean weak paid-acquisition economics.
4. **The next evidence threshold should be operating data, not more storefronts.** Useful proof would include active-member counts, cohort retention, pharmacy utilization, CAC, conversion rate, and employer deployments.
