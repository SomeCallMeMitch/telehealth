# LegUpRx / National Telehealth Providers

## Role in this project

LegUpRx is currently the leading **low-cost test platform** for launching a branded telehealth resale business quickly. Its economics are attractive, especially around primary care, family care, and the prescription program.

## Corporate / operational structure

Research to date indicates:

- LegUpRx operates under **Leg Up Recovery Franchising, LLC**.
- **National Telehealth Providers (NTP)** states publicly that it operates as a DBA of Leg Up Recovery Franchising.
- This means NTP does **not** appear to be an independent company that can simply be approached to bypass LegUp.
- LegUp-related terms/disclosures indicate that actual clinical services may involve separate healthcare organizations such as **Beluga Health, P.A.**, Lyric Health, NimbusRX, Bask Health, and affiliated physician networks depending on the service/program.

## Platform pricing observed

From the supplied LegUp pricing materials:

### Monthly plan
- $1 for first 7 days
- **$249/month** after trial
- Access to nationwide licensed physician/pharmacy network
- Elective medication offerings included
- One-time add-ons available

### One-time add-ons to monthly plan
- Care Services: **$199**
- Managed Website: **$199**
- DNA Testing: **$99**
- Testosterone Replacement Therapy: **$499**

### 12-month package
- **$2,499 one-time / 12 months**
- Includes Monthly Plan plus Care Services, managed website, DNA testing, and TRT

Family Primary Care covers up to seven family members at no extra cost plus the primary member, i.e. up to eight people total according to supplied materials.

## Attractive economics

Observed real-world resellers have priced similar LegUp/NTP care plans around:

- Individual Primary + Rx: approximately **$53-$60/month**
- Family + Rx: approximately **$149-$159/month**

At a $30 wholesale floor and $59 retail, gross spread is about **$29/member/month** before marketing/support/other costs.

At an $85 family wholesale floor and $159 retail, gross spread is about **$74/family/month** before costs.

## Major concern: customer / referral ownership

The current public LegUp Partner Agreement contains unusually important termination language. Research found language indicating that after termination:

- future commissions and rights to referred customers can be forfeited; and
- referrals remain property of LegUpRx.

This is the single biggest strategic concern discovered so far.

### Questions to resolve before scaling

1. If leads originate through our own app/site/CRM, do we retain ownership of their marketing contact data?
2. If LegUp terminates without cause, do commissions continue on customers we acquired?
3. Can we export customer contact information excluding PHI?
4. Can we market a replacement telehealth product to customers we originally acquired?
5. Can the agreement provide a 90-180 day transition period?
6. Can the agreement distinguish **clinical-record ownership** from **marketing/customer-relationship ownership**?
7. Can the customer/referral forfeiture language be modified by addendum?

## Public partner-count claims

LegUp-related pages have displayed inconsistent numbers such as 300+, 500+, and 700+ businesses. These may refer to different definitions or different dates, but should not be treated as a verified active-partner count.

Better diligence questions:

- How many currently paying partner businesses exist?
- How many had at least one transaction in the past 90 days?
- What percentage generated $500+ of commissions last month?
- Median monthly revenue per active partner?
- Median partner churn / tenure?

## Strategic position

LegUp currently looks useful as **Vendor #1 for validation**, not necessarily as the permanent backend.

Recommended posture:

- use LegUp to test market demand cheaply;
- own the brand, website, lead-gen tools, CRM, content, acquisition data, and analytics outside LegUp wherever possible;
- avoid building irreplaceable software tightly coupled to LegUp until ownership/termination issues are resolved;
- once traction exists, quote alternative infrastructure providers.
