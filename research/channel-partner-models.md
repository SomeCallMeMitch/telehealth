# Channel-Partner / Reseller-Recruitment Models

## Opportunity

A second business may be more scalable than simply reselling telehealth to consumers:

**Recruit businesses to become telehealth resellers/partners and earn recurring economics on the businesses brought in.**

LegUp currently offers roughly a **$500 referral fee** for bringing in a new partner business. That may be reasonable for a warm introduction, but it is weak compensation if we are responsible for building and operating a repeatable acquisition channel.

## Why recurring economics make more sense

If we build:
- funnels;
- vertical-specific landing pages;
- ROI calculators;
- outbound campaigns;
- ads;
- educational content;
- sales enablement;
- CRM sequences;
- onboarding tools;
- software that helps partners sell to their own customers;

...then we are creating a durable distribution channel, not simply making referrals.

In that case, compensation should ideally include some recurring component tied to the ongoing value of recruited partner accounts.

## Market precedent found

### TelehealthCash

Website: https://telehealthcash.com/

Observed public partner/affiliate economics included:
- compensation for qualified leads / signups;
- plus approximately **5% of profits for life** from partners brought into the system.

This is useful precedent because it appears to compensate people for recruiting **telehealth business operators**, not merely patients.

### Telegenix

Website: https://www.telegenix.com/

Observed public affiliate structure included:
- recurring percentage on patient orders/reorders;
- plus a smaller second-tier percentage on orders generated by affiliates recruited underneath the original affiliate.

This demonstrates that **multi-tier recurring overrides** already exist in the broader telehealth market.

### HelixMD

Website: https://helixmd.com/

Observed affiliate materials have advertised recurring percentage structures that increase with referred active-patient volume.

### MedStands

Website: https://medstands.com/

Observed public materials market:
- recurring per-active-patient compensation;
- lifetime/account-level attribution concepts;
- business/affiliate partner positioning;
- category-dependent economics.

Treat company-published payout claims as marketing until independently verified.

## Potential LegUp negotiation structure

Do **not** assume that patient-level referral compensation is legally appropriate. Healthcare referral compensation can raise federal/state issues depending on the services, payors, structure, and facts. Any negotiated model should be reviewed by qualified healthcare counsel and structured around legitimate marketing/channel services.

A possible business-to-business channel structure to discuss:

1. Existing **$500 activation bounty** for each new paying partner business.
2. Recurring share of the partner's **LegUp platform subscription** for as long as the recruited business remains active.
3. Possible additional percentage of LegUp's **net revenue/profit** attributable to the recruited partner, if counsel approves the structure.
4. Long-lived attribution (e.g. 24-36 months or lifetime while active).
5. Master channel-partner ID / reporting dashboard.
6. Visibility into recruited accounts, active/cancelled status, and commissions.
7. Contractual protection against LegUp terminating the channel relationship after a meaningful book of business is created.
8. Clear definition of what happens to residual commissions if the channel agreement ends.

## Negotiation framing

Suggested concept:

> The current $500 fee compensates an introduction. We are interested in building a repeatable acquisition channel for LegUp: vertical funnels, lead generation, sales enablement, onboarding, and partner growth. Comparable telehealth companies use ongoing overrides for channel partners. We would like to discuss a channel-partner agreement with recurring economics on businesses we originate.

- IV hydration / wellness clinics;
- insurance brokers;
- employee-benefit brokers;
- staffing agencies;
- small employers;
- 1099 / gig-worker communities;
- salons / aesthetics businesses;
- pharmacies;
- tax/accounting firms serving self-employed clients;
- membership associations;
- franchise systems;
- independent sales organizations.

## Key question

Can we build the **distribution engine independently of LegUp**?

Ideal answer: yes.

The platform should eventually be able to route qualified partners to whichever telehealth backend offers the best combination of:
- product fit;
- economics;
- customer ownership;
- service quality;
- compliance support;
- API/integration capability.

That would turn LegUp into Vendor #1 rather than make the entire business dependent on LegUp.
