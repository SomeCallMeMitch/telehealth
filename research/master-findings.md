# Master Findings

Last updated: September 15, 2026

## Executive summary

The opportunity currently appears to have **three layers**:

1. Launch a branded consumer telehealth membership using a turnkey backend.
2. Build a B2B channel that recruits other businesses to become telehealth resellers.
3. Over time, own the software/customer-acquisition layer and migrate to more direct clinical infrastructure if volume justifies it.

No provider has been selected. Research is still exploratory.

## Strongest current positives

- LegUpRx offers unusually low entry cost relative to many white-label telehealth competitors.
- The Primary Care + Prescription program has compelling economics at a $30/month wholesale floor.
- The Family + Rx plan at an $85/month wholesale floor may support attractive recurring margin if retail pricing around $149-$159 proves viable.
- The Rx formulary may be the best customer-acquisition hook because it converts existing prescription spend into a savings argument.
- A reseller ecosystem definitely exists, so the business model is operational rather than purely theoretical.
- Existing competitors demonstrate that recurring channel-partner economics are possible in this industry.
- Beluga and other infrastructure providers create a plausible path to owning more of the stack later.

## Strongest current concerns

- LegUp's current public partner-agreement language says future commissions/referral rights may be forfeited at termination and referrals remain LegUp property.
- Public claims about LegUp partner count are inconsistent and do not establish how many partners are actually successful.
- The $0 prescription benefit is attractive but needs deeper verification around caps, quantity limits, mail order, family utilization, and BestChoiceRx structure.
- Consumer CAC for low-cost telehealth membership is unknown.
- Many existing white-label brands appear small; we have not yet established strong examples of scale in the exact Primary Care + Rx model.
- Healthcare referral/channel compensation must be structured carefully with qualified counsel.

## Current business hypotheses

### Hypothesis A — Consumer membership

A strong acquisition proposition may be:

> "See if the prescriptions you already take are on the $0 list."

Then compare user-entered medication spend with a $59-ish individual membership or $149-$159 family membership that also includes telehealth access.

### Hypothesis B — Existing-audience partners

Rather than acquire consumers one by one, sell the capability to businesses that already have trusted audiences:

- gyms;
- med spas;
- IV/wellness businesses;
- insurance brokers;
- staffing agencies;
- small employers;
- membership organizations;
- salons/aesthetics businesses;
- gig-worker communities.

This may lower acquisition cost dramatically.

### Hypothesis C — B2B channel business

LegUp currently offers about $500 for a referred partner business. The stronger opportunity may be to negotiate:

- activation bounty;
- recurring share of platform revenue;
- possibly a counsel-approved override on LegUp economics generated by recruited businesses;
- protected attribution and reporting.

### Hypothesis D — Software layer

Build independent acquisition/partner software such as:

- Rx savings finder;
- family savings calculator;
- employer savings calculator;
- partner ROI calculator;
- co-branded landing pages;
- lead capture/CRM;
- QR campaigns;
- conversion tracking;
- sub-partner dashboards.

Long term, the software/distribution layer could work with multiple telehealth backends rather than one vendor.

## Important provider findings

### LegUpRx / National Telehealth Providers

- NTP is a DBA of Leg Up Recovery Franchising.
- LegUp is inexpensive and turnkey.
- Strong care-plan economics.
- Concerning customer/referral ownership language.

### Karpa Health

- Public messaging is substantially more ownership-friendly.
- Appears focused more heavily on elective/cash-pay medication programs.
- Exact customer/data rights must be confirmed in the signed plan agreement.

### Beluga Health

- Direct clinical-infrastructure provider for DTC telehealth brands.
- Could be a migration target after traction.
- Requires more integration and likely higher minimum economics than LegUp.

### TelehealthCash / MedStands / others

- Useful primarily as evidence that recurring partner/affiliate economics and business-recruitment models already exist in telehealth.
